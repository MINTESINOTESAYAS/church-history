\summarypart{NINE}{The Age of Global Expansion and Relocation}{1900--}
\begin{partoverview}
More people have become Christians in the last hundred years than at any other time; measured by the
evangelistic embrace of the faith, more may have happened in this century than in all the earlier
history of the church. The great missionary push of the late 1800s and early 1900s contributed to this
explosive growth, most of it south of the Equator. Yet the new growth has a character of its own and a
Spirit-given initiative of its own. Ironically, the former strongholds of Christian mission in Europe
and North America are meanwhile dormant or declining.

Two questions are left to history: whether the new centers of Christianity in the Global South and
beyond can keep a faithful Christian character and bear witness to this unprecedented work of the
Spirit, and whether signs of spiritual stirring will revive a faithful witness in the West. Without
such a stirring, the label ``post-Christian'' will fit the West better with each passing year.
\end{partoverview}
