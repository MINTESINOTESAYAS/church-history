\summarypart{SIX}{The Age of Reason and Revival}{1648--1789}
\begin{partoverview}
The quarrels of the Reformation were quarrels between believers over how a person is saved. The age
that followed asked whether revealed religion was needed at all, and it made science and reason, rather
than the Bible, the foundation of Western thought. The most effective Protestant reply was not a
counterargument. It was a series of revivals in which preachers called for a personal conversion and in
which the heart counted for more than the creed. Evangelical Christianity grew out of these awakenings
and spread through preaching, without the backing of governments. In the same years many believers
concluded that the faith could do without an established church, and they made their peace with
religious liberty.
\end{partoverview}
