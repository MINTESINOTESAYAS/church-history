\summarypart{SIX}{The Age of Reason and Revival}{1648--1789}
\begin{partoverview}
The Reformation age had been a debate among Christians about the way of salvation. The Age of Reason
denied supernatural religion altogether: respect for science and human reason took the place of the
Christian faith as the cornerstone of Western culture. Many Protestants met this crisis not with
arguments but with the experience of supernatural conversion, so that faith became less a matter of
dogma and more a matter of experience. This evangelical Christianity spread quickly by the power of
preaching alone. Many Christians also came to see that the support of the state was no longer needed
for the faith to survive; modern Christians could accept religious freedom.
\end{partoverview}
